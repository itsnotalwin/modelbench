EXHIBIT 01 — TIMELINE

Invention of grey

Before colour, there was tone. Seven moments when black, white and the miles between them did the work of representation — select a year to inspect it.

1826

The first photograph

Nicéphore Niépce exposes a pewter plate coated in bitumen for eight hours. The result is not a picture of the world in colour — it is a map of light and dark, grey by necessity.

What you are looking at: A bitumen plate. Eight hours of sun become four greys.

EXHIBIT 03 — PERCEPTION

Optical rooms

Grey is not only in the ink. It is made by the eye. Three rooms, three deceptions — each with its own controls, so you can break them and rebuild them.

Room A

Mach bands

A smooth gradient should look smooth. At its edges, the visual system exaggerates the change — and you see bands that are not there.

Lateral inhibition: neurons next to a bright edge suppress their neighbours. The brain reads a sharp step where the ramp says “almost nothing”. Lower the contrast and the bands vanish — proof they were never in the ink.

Room B

Hermann grid

White corridors cross black squares. Look straight at a crossing and the grey dots vanish; look away and they swim back.

In the periphery, each crossing is flanked by more white than a point inside the corridor, so inhibition fires unevenly. Fix your gaze on one crossing: it clarifies. The others, in your peripheral vision, stay grey.

Room C

Checker shadow

The two centre squares are the same grey — #808080. One sits on paper-white, one on shadow-black. Your eye insists they differ. It is lying.

A
B

Centre A and centre B are both #808080 — only their surrounds differ.

The brain discounts illumination and judges a grey by its neighbours. Adelson’s checker-shadow classic, reduced here to its essence: one grey, two surrounds. Reveal removes the surrounds and the illusion collapses.

EXHIBIT 06 — WORKSHOP

Type tester

Proof that one family, one colour and no decoration can still set a loud page. Bend the type here — size, weight, tracking, leading — and take the CSS with you.

Set in the same system stack as this page: Helvetica / Arial / sans-serif, with Georgia italics for emphasis. No webfont is loaded — that is the point.

ACHROMA

About

A fictional institute and a real website. The museum does not exist; the monochrome does — every pixel above was made without colour, in your browser, from code you can inspect.

Accessibility

Full keyboard operation, visible focus, live announcements for instruments, and a reduced-motion mode that stills every canvas. If your system asks for less motion, the site obeys.

Colophon

Set in Helvetica / Georgia / system mono. One HTML file, one stylesheet, one script. No frameworks, no fonts, no images, no tracking — it opens from disk and from any static host.
