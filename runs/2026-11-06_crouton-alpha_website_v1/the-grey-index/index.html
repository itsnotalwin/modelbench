03 / The Halftone Press

Pictures made of nothing.

Newspapers once printed photographs using only black ink and white paper, faking tone with dots. This press does the same live, on canvas. Choose a subject, tune the dots, download the result.

Registered at — dots. The “subject” is drawn by code, sampled pixel by pixel, then re-drawn as dots.

    06 / The Essay
    
      
    In praise of the middle.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Colour is a claim. Grey is a confession — it admits the light was never quite enough, and that you looked anyway.
    
      
    Every photograph you have ever loved was, for most of its history, a negotiation in grey. Ansel Adams, developing prints in a darkroom that smelled of vinegar and fixer, did not ask what colour the sky was. He asked how dark he dared make it before the paper held no more information — only grain, only the suggestion of weather. The Zone System he built with Fred Archer was not a theory of colour. It was a map of courage: ten zones from pure black to pure white, and the discipline to choose which zone a thing deserved to live in.
    
      
    Grey is also the colour of honest materials. Granite does not perform. Ash does not flatter. A dolphin’s back is the exact grey of the sea surface it disappears into, and an elephant’s hide is the grey of the road it walks at dusk. Nature rarely uses pure black or pure white outside of a few dramatic exceptions — a zebra, a magpie, the pupil of an eye — because pure values are promises, and the natural world prefers evidence. Grey is what evidence looks like before it is certain.
    
      
    
        
    “Black is the colour of things as they are. White is the colour of things as they might be. Grey is the colour of things as they are, being looked at.”
    
        — Studio note, The Grey Index, undated
      
    
      
    Architects learned this early. The concrete of a Brutalist façade, the slate of a Welsh roof, the fogged glass of a 1960s office — these surfaces were chosen not despite their greyness but because of it. Grey does not date the way a fashionable colour does. It absorbs change, weather, shadow and time, and asks only that you light it well. A grey wall is a page; what happens on it depends on the hour.
    
      
    And so this index, which contains no colour at all, is not a limitation. It is a controlled experiment. Remove hue, and what remains is structure: value, contrast, texture, legibility — the skeleton of every image and every interface. A designer who can make greys work can make anything work. A photographer who can print in black and white can see. The 256 steps between black and white are not a compromise. They are the whole scale, and everything worth doing happens somewhere along it.
    
      
    Start at 0. End at 255. Live somewhere in between, on purpose.
    
      
    
        
    Reading time: about 2 minutes.
