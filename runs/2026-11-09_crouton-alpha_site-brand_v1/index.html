How it works

Fifty minutes, held to heat.

We run it the way the old farm saunas ran it: steady fire, honest water, no itinerary. This is roughly what yours will feel like, minute by minute. You do not have to follow it — it is a shape to lean on.

  1. MIN 0–5 · ARRIVE

    Say hello, strip to comfort

    You park in the yard, find us by the smoke, hang your things on the line. Towels go on the bench. No faff, no forms unless you need us to know something.

  2. MIN 5–15 · FIRST HEAT

    Sit low, breathe slow

    Start on the lower bench — about 62°C. Let your shoulders come down. Doors open often. The tin cups sweat and so will you.

  3. MIN 15–25 · UP, THEN OUT

    Top bench, then cold

    When you are ready, the top bench — 82 to 88°C. Five minutes, or ten if you are steady. Then out to the plunge, in for ten seconds, no longer.

  4. MIN 25–40 · THE MIDDLE

    Round two, your way

    Back in, hotter or cooler as you like it. Jon or Mara will have eased the fire. Some people do three rounds. Some do one long sit and a lot of tea. Both count.

  5. MIN 40–50 · COOL AND GO

    Blanket, tea, valley air

    Last plunge if you fancy it, then the blanket bench behind the windbreak. Glow for an hour afterwards. Drive home the long way — people usually do.

A word on heat, because we owe you one: If you are pregnant, have a heart condition, or have been told to avoid heat or cold plunges, please do not come — or talk to your GP first and tell us in your booking notes. Feeling dizzy or odd at any point? Come out, sit down, tell us. We will think you sensible, not dramatic.

Who is behind it

A joiner, a horsebox, and a borrowed yard.

I am Mara Ellison, I am 44, and until 2021 I fitted kitchens for a living. In 2020 my dad died and left me the horsebox he had meant to restore “one day”. I could not bear to sell it, so I lined it, built two benches, cut a step into the side, and had a sauna inspector tell me, kindly, that it was the best bad idea he had ever signed off.

We lit the first fire in a borrowed corner of Birch Hill Farm in October 2021. Six neighbours came. Two had never done a sauna, one was my mum, and the plunge barrel iced over before the end of the third set. I knew then it was not a business plan — it was a thing people wanted to come back to.

Jon — my brother, ex-postie, back man — looks after the fire on the days I cannot. He is better at it than I am, and he writes the weather in the log book in pencil so you can see where he changed his mind. Between us we still split every log, still refill the barrel between sets, and still think you should sit low first. If we ever get too posh for that, sell the box and buy us a boat.

Our promise, in writing: if the fire goes out or the heat is not right, your set is free or rebooked — your call. If we cannot hold your set for any reason, we tell you the same day, not the same evening.

Fair questions

Asked at the door, answered straight.

Is it mixed? Can I book a set without strangers?

The 50 is shared — up to four guests, mixed or not, whatever turns up. If you want no strangers, book the Private Box (£95); it is just your party. We never mix a Private Box with anyone else, not even for five minutes. If you book The 50 and the set is already full, we tell you straight away and offer another time.

What do I bring? What do I wear?

Bring a towel (or hire ours for £3) and something to change into. Inside the box, most people wear swimwear; some wear nothing at all. Both are fine and nobody keeps score. Leave rings and watches in the car — metal gets hot. We have hooks but they are plastic for a reason.

What if it rains, or the wind is awful?

We run in most weather — the box is warm and dry and the plunge does not mind rain one bit. If a set would be unsafe (high wind, lightning close by, flood on the lane) we cancel it and email you the same day with a free rebook or refund. Askwith Grange on a moor wind is the one to watch; we will always tell you if we are worried.

Can I get a shower afterwards?

Not properly, no. You get the cold jug, a bucket, and the yard. Most people say they prefer it by the second visit — the sweat rinses off, the pores shut, and you are done. If you need a proper wash before work, come to an early set and use the farm’s tap by the parlour (Birch Hill and High Winder only).

What is the cancellation and payment policy?

You pay on arrival — cash or card (the reader lives in the cab, not in the field, so give it a minute in April). Cancel or move up to 24 hours before your set and nothing is owed. Inside 24 hours we ask for half the set price, because there is a fire laid and a bench that could have sold. If we cancel, you owe nothing and get first pick of the next week. Farm Cards are stamped, not charged, on the day.

I am pregnant / have a heart condition / take drugs that affect heat. Can I come?

Honestly: probably not, and we would rather you asked us than guessed. Sauna heat and cold plunges put real strain on the heart and blood pressure, and pregnancy changes the picture again. If any of that applies, talk to your GP first and tell us in the booking notes what they said. We will happily hold your money for a later visit, or give it back — no paperwork, no fuss.

Are children allowed? Are dogs?

Children over 12 are welcome in a Private Box with an adult, at the same price — the heat is real and we do not take younger ones in the box. Dogs on a lead are welcome in the yards (they usually prefer the blanket bench to the plunge) but not in the box. If your dog is nervous of steam, they will tell you before we do.
