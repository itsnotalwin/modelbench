The problem

Hard walls turn small rooms into echo boxes.

Plaster and glass reflect sound. In a living room or home office that means voices get harder to follow, calls sound thin, and music never settles. Loamline panels sit where the bounce happens — behind the sofa, beside the desk, around the record player.

We make each panel to order in our workshop: timber reclaimed from local demolition, grown-mycelium core, and untreated wool felt. No foam dust, no black plastic — just honest layers you can see and touch.

Materials & process

Three layers. Nothing to hide.

Frame — salvaged softwood

Each 120 × 40 cm module is framed in reclaimed pine and oak offcuts, sanded smooth and finished with hardwax oil. Timber grain varies — that’s the point. Every batch is numbered.

Core — grown mycelium

The acoustic heart is a panel of mycelium grown on agricultural waste, then dried and compressed. It’s light, resilient and compostable at end of life. We press it to 40 mm — thick enough to matter, thin enough for a hallway.

Skin — untreated wool felt

A 6 mm natural wool felt, stapled and trimmed by hand. It takes the scratches, keeps the dust down and softens the look. Choose natural, charred or plant-dyed colourwash finishes.

Care & provenance

Made to be kept, not replaced.

  • Vacuum wool with a soft brush monthly.
  • Oil timber frames yearly with hardwax oil.
  • Keep panels dry — they’re for interiors.
  • At end of life: timber and mycelium are compostable; wool is recyclable as insulation.
