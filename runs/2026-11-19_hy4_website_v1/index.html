01 / ARGUMENT

A SHORT DEFENCE OF UNCERTAINTY

A CASE FOR THE MIDDLE.

“Black closes the door. White floods the room. Grey leaves a chair out for whoever is still asking questions.”

NO HUE HAS BEEN ADDED, REMOVED, OR IMPLIED.

※

I. THE LIMITS

Absolutes are efficient. A black mark is either there or it is not; a white page makes no demand. Both are useful precisely because they refuse to negotiate.

II. THE MIDDLE

Between them are 254 possible answers. Each one changes the weight of a line, the depth of a room, the temperature of a face. None of them needs a name to be felt.

III. THE EYE

The eye is not a meter. It borrows context, remembers the last thing it saw, and invents an edge where none exists. This publication is built for that unreliable instrument.

When two greys meet exactly, the boundary between them stops existing. What remains is a single plane and the faint suspicion that your screen has lied to you.

05 / COLOPHON

THE SMALL PRINT, SET LARGE

HOW THIS EDITION WAS MADE.

CONCEPT

A one-page publication that treats grey as a subject rather than a background. The oversized type, hairline rules and marginal notes borrow from scientific plates and newspaper supplements.

TECHNIQUE

Every illustration is generated in Canvas or authored as SVG. The halftone field, moiré rings, contours, rain, folds and eclipse are deterministic grayscale arithmetic — no image files, libraries, fonts or network requests.

ACCESS

Native links, buttons and form controls; visible focus states; live results; descriptive canvas labels; a skip link; arrow-key plate and swatch navigation; reduced-motion defaults; and a printable stylesheet.

WITH MORE TIME

I would add an exportable print folio of the six plates, a screen-reader-readable luminance graph, and automated visual checks across display calibrations. The instruments would also gain a no-script fallback.
