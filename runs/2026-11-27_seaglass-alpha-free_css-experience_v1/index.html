01 On measuring the heavens

Before photography, before the telescope, people measured the sky by eye and by angle. An orrery is an answer to a simpler question: if we cannot visit these worlds, can we at least hold their arrangement in our hands?

Every turning ring here keeps true time. The instrument is a argument in brass — that the heavens are orderly, and that order can be shown.

02 Why a model at all

A model is honest about being a model. ORRERY does not claim to be Asteria; it claims to show its proportions, its periods, its families. Where the record is uncertain, the plate says so.

The proportions here are compressed for legibility — no orrery could turn Kelvar on the same scale as Cinder — but the order, the direction, and the nesting are true.

03 The Asteria system

Four worlds circle a single amber dwarf. Cinder, hot and bare, hugs the star. Maren, a teal ocean, sits in the temperate band. Kelvar, a ringed giant, dominates the outer reaches. Pall, a pale icy dwarf, trails on a wide tilted path.

The details on each plate — periods, distances, temperatures — are consistent across the Instrument and Catalog views. The same numbers appear wherever a body is shown.

04 Clockwork & finish

On a real orrery a concealed train of gears carries each world at its own rate. Here the train is a single CSS keyframe: one turn, smoothly, forever, each body at its own period and starting angle.

Brass, jewelled pivots, and engraved plates are described — and then re-created — in CSS gradients, masks and shadows. No images, no fonts to download, no script. The whole instrument is a single document.

05 Reading the plates

Each body carries a plate. Figures are given to a sensible precision and rounded where the source tradition would round them; “~” marks an approximation. Asteria, the star, has no orbital period of its own and no parent body.

Distances are mean orbital radii in astronomical units (1 AU = the Earth–Sun distance, here measured from Asteria). Diameters are equatorial. Temperatures are approximate surface values. All system data is invented for this instrument but kept internally consistent.

Colophon · access & honesty

Workshop

How this instrument is made, who can use it, and what it does not do.

Built with HTML & CSS only

There is no JavaScript in this document — not for animation, not for state, not for layout. Every interactive state is a native form control or a URL target; every motion is a CSS transition or animation. The page works with scripts disabled.

  • Grid & Flexbox for the app shell, ledger and readouts
  • Custom properties for theming, per-body data and motion
  • Container queries so cards adapt to their own width
  • Logical properties for direction-independent spacing
  • Masks, gradients, filters for glass, glow, vignette and planets
  • Transforms for the orbital train and hover/focus motion
  • Modern selectors :has(), :is(), :where(), :not(), :nth-child(), :focus-within
  • Native behaviour radio groups, checkboxes, <details>/<summary>, anchors, <button type="reset">

Motion & stillness

Orbits, glows, entrances and theme changes are a considered motion language on shared easing and duration tokens. Two independent ways to calm it:

  • Your system prefers-reduced-motion setting is honoured automatically.
  • The Orbital rate → Still control pauses the train by hand.

Reduced motion keeps every state and control; it only removes movement.

Keyboard & touch

Everything reachable by mouse is reachable by keyboard and touch.

  • Tab / Shift+Tab move between controls
  • ← ↑ → ↓ move within a radio group; Space selects
  • Space / Enter open <details> and follow links
  • Visible focus rings on every control; large touch targets; logical order
  • Orbiting bodies have equivalent, steady controls in the body dial; nothing depends on hover

State, reversibly

No hidden state, no traps. Every choice can be undone.

  • Views, theme, body, rate, telescope, filters and sort are all reversible
  • Reset instrument (<button type="reset">) restores defaults — no script
  • Field Notes anchors are real links; Back returns
  • Theme works independently of your OS setting

Honest limits

  • CSS-only: there is no sound, no persistence between visits, and no dynamic text output. Choosing a body reveals its prepared plate; it does not compute new data.
  • Richer transitions (@starting-style, discrete display transitions, scroll-driven timelines, ::details-content) are progressive enhancements; the experience is complete without them.
  • Advanced selectors :has() and container queries require a current browser (2024+). No fallback is provided for very old engines.
  • Orbiting labels are a convenience; the steady body dial is the accessible path. Under reduced motion, bodies sit at their starting angles.
  • The Asteria system is fictional; its figures are internally consistent invention, not measurement.
