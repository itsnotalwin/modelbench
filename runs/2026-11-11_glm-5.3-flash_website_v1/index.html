FIELD ATLAS Nº 01 · THE SLEEPING CAR

NOCTURNE

Once, the night was a mode of transport. You handed your body to a city terminus at dusk, and it gave you back at dawn — eight hundred kilometres away, with different weather in the window. This atlas keeps the timetable of that idea: twelve sleeper services, 1883 to tonight, drawn as one diagram and read as elegies.

OUTLOOK
Revived as expensive specials; the real thing died with the 1977 season.

The first train to carry its own name like a flag, and the one that taught Europe that borders were something you could sleep through. Its later years were shabbier than its legend — the through carriages were nicknamed “the Polytechnique” for the student traffic — and in 1977 the last Wagons-Lits service was withdrawn. Everything sold today as luxury rail was invented in these carriages.

04 — THE ELEGIES

Four pieces for the sleeping car

Every atlas of the night needs its marginalia. These are ours.

ELEGY I

The Border at Four A.M.

There is a genre of waking that only trains provide. The corridor lights come up like interrogation, boots go past in threes, and a voice with a different consonant inventory asks, through the glass, for the thing you left in your other coat. You sign your pulse rate onto a form. Outside, a station you will never be able to name again is holding its breath with you; the dog on platform two is the only witness both countries will agree on. Then the window goes dark, the train takes up its slack, and somewhere behind you a border quietly closes like a book being returned to its shelf. Sleep, when it comes back, has a stamp in it.

ELEGY II

In Praise of the Dining Car

The dining car is the last truly public room. The tables are laid for people who have not met yet. The waiter knows the train better than the driver: he can tell, by the tilt of the soup, which valley you are in and which tunnel is coming. At midnight the menu shortens and the lights come down a stop, and the carriage performs its best trick — it makes motion feel like permanence. Cities slide past uncriticised. You order the last thing on the list, the wheel rhythm keeps the time, and for the length of a night the world is a set table on a long straight rail.

ELEGY III

On Waking Somewhere

The trick of the sleeper is that it steals a day’s travel from the hours you were not using anyway. But its real gift is the waking: the grey light that is not your grey light, the curtain gap framing a landscape nobody chose for you, the sudden arithmetic of where am I performed with a dry mouth. Hotels promise you the same room everywhere; the sleeper promises the opposite — that you will open your eyes somewhere else, and know it before you know it. Geography becomes something that happened to you while you were unconscious, which is, if you think about it, how most of the important things arrive.

ELEGY IV

A Glossary of Night Noises

COUPLING, n.
A deep clank that starts three carriages away and arrives like swallowed thunder; the train, taking up its slack, clears its throat.
WHEEL RHYTHM, n.
The metronome under the mattress. Four beats, a rail joint, silence, four beats. The only clock you will not check.
THE COACH DOOR, n.
A pneumatic sigh, opened by someone else’s mistake at two in the morning.
BERTH CURTAIN, n.
A small brass argument, settled by whoever is tireder.
BORDER LIGHTS, n.
The corridor coming up bright enough to read passports by. Not your passport. Not your problem, for nine more minutes.
DAWN BLIND, n.
The strip of grey under the curtain that tells you the latitude better than any signboard.

05 — COLOPHON

Method, type, colour, engineering

METHOD
Facts compiled from timetables, guidebooks, operator announcements and memory. Times, distances and dates are indicative rather than authoritative — this is an atlas, not an audit. The board’s clatter is synthesized on the spot with the Web Audio API; there are no recordings on this page.
TYPE
Set in the system grotesque, a monospaced face, and Georgia italic. No fonts were downloaded; the board flips in the same type as the timetable.
COLOUR
Black, white, and the greys between. Night trains run through the dark, and so does this page.
ENGINEERING
One HTML file, one stylesheet, one script. No frameworks, no build step, no external requests. The split-flap board is DOM and transforms; the diagram is hand-drawn SVG; all motion obeys the visitor’s reduced-motion preference.
ACCESSIBILITY
Keyboard operable throughout, with a skip link, visible focus, live announcements for board changes, and contrast checked on every grey.
