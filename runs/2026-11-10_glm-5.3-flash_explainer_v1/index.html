The model, honestly stated

Everything on screen — every transform, curve, readout and particle — is computed from a single crank angle through the equations below (js/model.js is the only source of truth). It is a zero-dimensional, teaching-grade engine model in the tradition of Heywood: trends, orders of magnitude and cycle events are engine-accurate; absolute values are indicated (before friction) and idealised.

Slider-crank
x(θ) = r(1−cos θ) + ℓ − √(ℓ² − r² sin²θ), with r = 43 mm, ℓ = 150 mm. Torque T = Fgas·dx/dθ. Inertia option: mrec = 0.62 kg, F = −m·a(θ).
Valve gear
lift = Lmax·sin1.5(π·s). Intake 708°→220° (232°, 9.2 mm), exhaust 495°→8° (233°, 8.0 mm). Cams rotate at exactly ½ crank speed.
Compression / expansion
Polytropic: P = PIVC·(VIVC/V)n, n = 1.32 unburned, 1.25 burned. Effective compression ratio follows from IVC at 220° (≈ 9.4 : 1 at 10.5 : 1 geometric).
Combustion
Wiebe: xb = 1 − exp(−5·s³), duration 40°, start = spark. Burn-phasing penalty on peak pressure; empirical wall-heat decay during expansion.
Gas exchange
Manifold pressure from throttle (0.28–0.98 bar abs), mid-stroke intake depression, blow-down exponential to 1.08 bar back-pressure, pumping loop included in net IMEP.
Knock
Heuristic: peak > 72 bar, or high load + advance ≥ 26° + CR ≥ 11 → ring-down pressure oscillation added after the peak (visible on the P–θ chart).
Simplifications
Steady-state speed (no flywheel ripple), no residual-gas dynamics, no friction (indicated values), single-zone burn, ideal gas. Fault modes are physically motivated approximations.
