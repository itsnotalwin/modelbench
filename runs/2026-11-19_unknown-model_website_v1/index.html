Prologue

There is no such thing as silence.

Even in an anechoic chamber, you hear your own nervous system. Between any two stations there is noise — hiss, crackle, the residue of the universe cooling down. This is not silence. It is the sound of everything happening at once, unpatterned and unowned.

What follows is a field guide to the in-between: a collection of small, quiet machines that make only shades of grey. There are no images loaded from servers. Every mark on every screen was drawn by a formula, right here, as you watch.

The dial works. The static is audible if you enable it. Drag the slider or use your arrow keys. Nothing is being sent anywhere. Nothing is being saved. When you close this page, every pattern below disappears without a record.

Manifesto for listening to nothing

We keep building
louder worlds and then
wondering where
the quiet went.

There is a tendency to treat noise as an error. It is the thing you subtract to get the signal, the residue between the intended notes. But noise is older than signals. It predates radio, predates speech, predates the Earth itself. The cosmic microwave background is noise left over from the beginning of time — the oldest thing we can hear, and it sounds like a television between channels.

We built this page in that spirit. There is nothing to buy, nothing to subscribe to, nothing to optimise. There are only patterns that exist for as long as your tab is open. They are made of integers and time. They look slightly different for every visitor and they will never look this way again.

“The sky is only blue because of noise. A perfect atmosphere would be black. We are all looking at a field of scattered light and calling it a day.” — Attributed, possibly apocryphal

If you leave the dial open, you will find stations that aren't there. Some are the mixing products of two real stations heterodyning in the receiver. Some are the power supply humming. Some are your own tinnitus, made audible by the silence. The point of a receiver is not that it receives signals. The point of a receiver is that it gives you a place to stand while everything else crashes through.

Every broadcast ends in static,
but the static never ends.
