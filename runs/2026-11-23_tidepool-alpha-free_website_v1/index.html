Issue 03 · a paper study

The Press House

A study in paper, type and the slow machinery that turns one into the other. Written and drawn inline — every shade is grey, every line is black or white, and nothing comes from anywhere else.

01 · pulp

What pulp was

Before anything is pressed, the page is a mass of short fibers in a bath of water.

Paper began as pulp. A beater drove the fibers down to a slurry; the mold set a mesh of them; the press lifted the water out of the sheet. Rags were the raw material for centuries, and the sheet that had served its purpose was torn down and turned again.

Three things mattered in that bath: the beater, the setter of the mold, and the press that dripped. The world called the result paper, and the sheet only knew itself as the second life of another.

Every sheet of type grew from a pulp bath; the ink sat on the surface, and the water left beneath it.

03 · sheets

Sheets of type, cut from one edition

Each face draws its sheet inline; activating a face opens a note on how that sheet was made.

  • Stipple

    A sheet on which every mark is a dot; there is no line, only the pattern of the dots and the paper between them.

  • Skeleton

    A ruled sheet reduced to the minimum of structure: a handful of lines in both directions, leaving the reading of the paper to the margins.

  • Word cut

    The word PRESS cut from the sheet: black paper with the type cut from it, figure and ground inverted in the simplest form.

  • Folio

    A folded sheet of the book: the body and the fold, the crease and the margin of the gutter.

04 · ages

The page, from stalk to sheet

  1. 3000 BCE

    Papyrus, Egypt: the first sheets, made from the stalk of the marsh reed, pressed flat. The page is a surface of light paper, not yet paper at all.

  2. 1150

    Xàtiva, Spain: the first European paper mill, under the Moors. The sheet arrived from the Islamic world, and it was not yet in the books of the West.

  3. 1293

    Fabriano, Italy: watermarks, a signature written in water, are invented here. The maker's mark on the sheet.

  4. 1450

    The printing press: paper, once rare and costly, becomes a commodity. Pages are set in type and run through the press again and again.

  5. 1806

    The continuous paper machine (Fourdrinier): the sheet never stops being made; speed and length grow with it.

  6. 1867

    The web rotary press (Bullock): news and book stacks run at speed; the world of the page moves with them.

  7. 1971

    The laser printer (Xerox PARC): type returns set by light; the plate is gone and the beam sets it again, page by page.
