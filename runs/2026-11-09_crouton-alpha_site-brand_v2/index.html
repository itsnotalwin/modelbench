What a day looks like with Badgerbook

You buy a system that makes the first hour of the day feel normal. At seven, the receptionist opens one view: today’s appointments, the gaps, the animals due a chaser, and the weight of the person walking in with a cocker spaniel. Nothing is red. Nothing is shouting. The day is sequenced before anyone has had coffee, and the coffee is not held hostage by a login error.

We built Badgerbook because the practice we started with had four systems and no memory. The lab portal was in one tab, the billing in another, and the clinical history in a file that the last vet had named “FINAL_v2”. When you are in a consult, you should not be proving which tab is true. You should be looking at the dog.

So we made one record. One timeline, one problem list, one weight chart that does not lie. If a lab result arrives, it lands in the same timeline and the vet who ordered it is notified. If a client rings, the note is dated, attributed and visible to the next person who answers. That sounds obvious. In our experience, obvious is what practices are missing.

Who it is for

  • Independent practices of 2 to 15 vets that do not want a corporate roll-out.
  • Mixed and farm practices that need large-animal visit notes on a phone in a yard.
  • Referral and specialist centres that need referral letters without the five-tab dance.

We do not sell to corporate groups looking for a chain-wide re-platform. If your group needs that, you have our respect and our best wishes, and you should speak to someone else.

Records, billing and the spaces between

Three things break small practices: records nobody can find, invoices nobody sends, and the chaser that is always two days late. Badgerbook fixes the middle one by making it boring. An invoice is drafted from the consult, not after it. The estimate is the invoice, adjusted. The client sees the estimate, agrees, and the bill is the estimate not a surprise. That one habit changes the temper of a whole practice.

Clinical notes that write themselves — carefully

Templates you edit, not a dictation gimmick. A vaccine template knows the last dose. A dental template knows the chart. You tick, you amend, you sign. The record is yours, not a suggestion from a model that has never held a cat.

Billing at the point of care

Stock, discounts and cat-dog-weight-band pricing are set once. The bill is ready when the animal leaves the room. If a client queries it later, the audit trail is one click, not a scroll through three systems and a prayer.

Reminders that are not nagging

Vaccines, parasite control and weight checks go out by SMS and email, and stop when the animal is booked. You control the wording. An owner who is chased by a person sounds different from one chased by a system, and your clients can tell.

Proof from practices like yours

At Mill Lane Veterinary Surgery, a four-vet mixed practice in Yorkshire, the partners told us the first week would decide it. By day six, the reception team had stopped printing the day list. By day twenty, the practice manager had recovered three hours a week that had been spent printing it. At month three, outstanding invoices had fallen by a third, because invoices were being sent in the room, not in the evening.

At Harbour Vets in Newlyn, a two-vet seafront practice, the gain was quieter. The vets stopped carrying paper into consults. Lab results for a diabetic cat were in the timeline before the owner rang to ask. The owner, who had expected to chase, said she had “never been rung first”. That is the kind of surprise we are aiming for.

We publish our support data because we ask to be judged on it. Average first response is eleven minutes in clinic hours. The median practice is live in nine days. The few practices that leave do so because they have been bought by a group, not because the system failed them, and we keep the data export door open for exactly that reason.

Questions from the practice meeting

How long does migration take? Nine days median. We migrate animal and client records, outstanding invoices and recurring reminders. The old system stays read-only for a year, which is how you check we did it properly.

Do you charge per transaction? No. A practice that is charged per text will ration texts, and then animals miss vaccines. We charge for the practice, not for the owner.

What if we are bought by a group? Your data exports in standard formats, and we will help you leave well. We have done it twice. It took a week and nobody lost a record.

Security and support

Backups are hourly, encrypted, and tested monthly. If the practice internet goes down, Badgerbook continues in offline mode and syncs later — because a rural practice in a storm should not depend on a fibre cabinet. Support is answered by the team that built the workflow, and we use the words “I can see” in the first minute.
