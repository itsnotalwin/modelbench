The room

A sail loft with
a fire in the middle.

We kept the beams and the harbor light from the old Okafor sail loft, and put a wood hearth where the cutting table used to be. Twenty-eight seats. No freezer. One long table for whoever shows up alone.

Marrow & Tide started with a bad lease and a good fire. In 2019, chef Nell Calloway and baker Sam Okafor took over a drafty sail loft two streets up from the fish pier, patched the roof, and built a hearth out of Maine fieldstone. The first menu was four things written on the back of a tide chart. People came anyway.

Seven years later the room is still small on purpose. The fish comes from six boats we know by name. The vegetables come from three farms within an hour's drive. The bread is mixed at four in the morning with a starter we call Brine, because it smells like the harbor and refuses to die. There is no freezer in the building. If we run out, we run out.

“We cook what the boats bring and what the garden gives. If it isn't good enough to eat with our hands, it doesn't leave the pass.”

— Nell Calloway, chef & partner
