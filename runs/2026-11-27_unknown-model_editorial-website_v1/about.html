About Palimpsest

What this is

Palimpsest is an editorial journal about writing, erasure, and what remains. Its subject is the layered document: pages scraped and reused, texts recovered from underneath, remarks added at the edges, and the difference between what is visible and what is preserved. Volume I, Number 1, The Undertext issue, contains two full-length essays and this colophon. See the archive for contents.

Everything here is original. No article reproduces an existing publication’s layout or voice; no copy is lifted from other sources; all artwork is original inline SVG or CSS; there are no photographs, no hotlinked images, and no external assets.

Editorial point of view

We read documents as layered objects, not transparent windows. Every text carries an undertext: what was on the page before it, what was scraped away, what has been added around it, and what each reader leaves behind. Erasure is evidence, not absence. A margin is a conversation, not decoration. A reconstruction is a claim about what remains, not about what was meant.

This point of view has practical consequences. We prefer small verifiable claims to grand ones; we name what we are describing and say plainly when something is reconstructed, inferred, or unrecovered; we distinguish what a document contains from what a scholar concludes; and we treat both the object and its history with care. We do not pretend certainty we do not have.

Voice

Our voice is scholarly but unhurried; precise but not cold. Sentences are clear and mostly short. Jargon is defined on first use (see e.g. glossae, rasura, multispectral imaging in the articles). We write in the first person plural when speaking for the journal (“we”) and keep article bylines for individual authors. We avoid hype, urgency, and rhetorical questions as filler. We say “we don’t know” when we don’t.

Structure

Every article has a kicker, headline, standfirst, byline, publication date, reading time, section, issue, tags, a contents list, a body with named sections, figures with captions, margin notes, footnotes with back-links, and related reading. The homepage leads with the issue’s feature, lists sections, and states the editorial point of view. The archive lists everything. Navigation is identical on every page.

Colophon

Set in system serif typefaces (with Palatino/Iowan Old Style where available), system sans-serif for metadata and navigation, and monospace for code—no webfonts are loaded, so nothing shifts as the page renders. Body measure is capped near 65 characters; a wide-margin layout places reader-facing margin notes in a side column on large screens and inline on small ones. Spacing follows a half-rem rhythm with a fluid gutter. Original artwork is inline SVG and pure CSS.

  • Foundation: semantic HTML5 + CSS3. No React, Vue, Angular, or component frameworks.
  • JavaScript: none. The publication is fully readable and navigable without it; the responsive menu is a native <details>/<summary> disclosure.
  • Motion: no animation; prefers-reduced-motion is respected and smooth scrolling is disabled for it.
  • Layout shift: system fonts, sized inline SVG, stable spacing; nothing loads late.
  • Print: a print stylesheet hides navigation and preserves article structure.

Accessibility

Semantic landmarks (header, nav, main, article, section, aside, footer); a skip link first in tab order; one h1 per page with ordered headings; visible :focus-visible outlines; keyboard-operable native controls; aria-current on the active page; aria-labels on navigation regions; aria-labelledby wiring; inline SVG figures with title/desc and role="img"; decorative elements hidden; descriptive alt-equivalent text; figure captions; footnote links that go both ways; lang attributes; sufficient color contrast; prefers-reduced-motion. The site is designed to work at 320px width, without JavaScript, and with keyboard only.

Contact & letters

Palimpsest is published as a self-contained static site. There is no newsletter, no comment system, no submission form, and no mailing address to offer—nothing here pretends to collect your data. Letters and corrections are not processed by this edition. If a factual claim in an article is wrong, the right recourse is to read the cited note and judge the evidence; every claim is meant to be checkable.
