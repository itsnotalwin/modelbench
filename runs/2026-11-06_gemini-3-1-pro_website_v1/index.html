Section IV · Psychophysics & Halftone Geometry

The Mathematics of Grey

Achromatic perception is governed by logarithmic sensory compression, lateral inhibition across tonal boundaries, and spatial integration of binary ink dots. Experiment directly with the three foundational laws below.

EXPERIMENT 4.1 · WEBER–FECHNER LAW

Perceptual Gamma (γ = 2.2) vs. Linear Radiance

Human vision perceives brightness logarithmically: doubling physical photon flux feels like a single constant step. Encoding 8-bit grey linearly wastes half of all 256 values on the brightest stop, starving shadows into visible contour bands and Mach-band illusions.

EXPERIMENT 4.2 · ZONE SYSTEM ANCHORS

The 11-Zone Surface Material Atlas

Each Zone represents a 1-stop (2×) change in scene luminance, anchored around Zone V (18% neutral reflectance). Full physical texture exists only between Zones II and VIII; Zones 0–I and IX–X mark the thresholds of pure silver black and baryta paper base.

EXPERIMENT 4.3 · 1-BIT SPATIAL HALFTONING

Simulating Continuous Tone with Pure Black & White

Lithographic presses and e-ink matrices possess only two states: 0% ink and 100% ink. By dispersing quantization residuals across neighboring pixels (Floyd–Steinberg) or modulating periodic dot radii (Ives screen), the retina integrates binary patterns into smooth grey.
