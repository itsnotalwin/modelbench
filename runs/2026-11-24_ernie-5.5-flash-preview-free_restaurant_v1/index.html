01La MaisonThe House

A house
built on tide.

In 1926 Augustine Le Goff, widow of a sailmaker, turned her husband's workshop into a comptoir. She served the oysters that the Cancale boats carried up through the Porte Saint-Vincent each morning, on beds of crushed ice, with salted butter and rye bread. The sailors called the place le vert-de-gris — after the copper locks the sea air had eaten green.

Four generations later, her great-granddaughter Maëlle keeps the same rules: shuck to order, never reheat what swam yesterday, and let the tide decide what arrives. Everything on the menu travels less than forty kilometres — except the wine, which comes from the Loire and is the only thing here allowed to be far from home.

There are sixty-two seats, eleven wines by the glass, and a zinc counter worn into a shallow curve by a century of elbows. If the weather turns, we close the windows and light the candles. If it doesn't, we leave them open and let the harbour in.

“An oyster cannot be explained. It has to be opened.” Maëlle Le Goff — chef & fourth generation
