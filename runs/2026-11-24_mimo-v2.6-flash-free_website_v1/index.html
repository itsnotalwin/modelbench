00

Prelude

Colour is a decision. Grey is a fact.

Take the colour away and nothing that mattered is lost. What survives is the only information a shadow, a printed page or a black-and-white photograph has ever carried: how much light, and where. Structure. Weight. Distance. Attention. Everything colour is usually asked to fix is usually asked to fix structure.

Achroma is a field guide to that constraint, and an instrument panel for it. Every image on this page — the grain over the type, the printed plate, the sphere being taken apart one decision at a time — is computed in your browser from a little arithmetic. No stock photography, no imported fonts, no external requests. If something here looks photographic, it is because light behaves the same way whether or not you paint it.

Five instruments follow, in order: a ladder you can climb, a dot you can count, an error you can see, a shadow you can move, and a record of how it was all made. Turn the page.

The Ladder

Twenty-four steps between nothing and everything.

Ansel Adams called it the zone system: evenly spaced steps from paper white to maximum black, and the whole craft of the print lying in knowing which step a thing belongs to. The ladder is not decoration on a darkroom wall. It is the ruler.

These steps are evenly spaced in code value, which is not the same thing as evenly spaced in perceived light — a fact that bites every time. So the readout gives you the number, the hex, the perceptual luminance and the honest contrast ratio against this page. A value without its ratio is a rumour.

02

The Dot

An image you can count.

A newspaper has never printed grey. It printed black dots on white paper at densities the eye agreed to average, and the agreement held right up until you held it up to the light. Halftone is a contract between a printer and a viewer, renewed at every distance.

Below, a landscape is resolved into one shape per cell. The cell is the resolution of the contract; the angle is the oldest trick in the trade — rotate the screen and the dots stop lining up into moiré. Move the controls and watch a picture dissolve back into arithmetic.

The Error

The mistake that looks like a photograph.

Two levels, one decision per pixel: on or off. The naive version — a flat threshold — is a cliff, and everything on one side falls off it together. Dithering is the trick of spreading the debt: decide wrongly here, and owe the difference to your neighbours.

Floyd and Steinberg paid it forward along the scanline in seventeenths. Bayer paid it out of a fixed matrix of thresholds tiled like a mosaic, which is why ordered dithering has a rhythm you can actually see. Both are running below on the same shaded sphere: one light, one direction, one floor, two colours.

The Shadow

Form is what light does on the way past an object.

A sphere is a circle until something illuminates it. The moment light arrives from a direction the circle acquires a terminator, a core shadow, a lit edge bounced back from the floor, and a cast shadow that anchors it — and the eye stops seeing geometry and starts seeing a thing.

The word below is built from forty stacked copies of itself, each one a step further from the paper's tone. That is how an extruded letterform is drawn, and it is also, more or less, how a shadow is made.

Penumbra

05

The Record

Colophon, and an honest account of the making.

This page is a single static folder: one HTML file, one stylesheet, one script, one favicon. It opens from a disk, from a repository subfolder or from any static host with nothing to install and nothing to build.

Imagery
Three images are computed rather than fetched: the film grain over the page (a tiled noise pattern redrawn ten times a second), the halftone plate (a landscape built from gradients and summed sine ridges, then screened cell by cell), and the sphere study (Lambert shading plus a soft cast shadow, then thresholded, matrix-dithered or error-diffused in place). The dial in the masthead is hand-written SVG.
Type
A system serif for the argument, a system monospace for the instruments. Nothing is downloaded, so nothing blocks paint and no third party is asked to identify you.
Palette
One hue, deliberately absent. Every value on the page — type, rules, shadows, gradients, focus rings, favicon — is a shade of grey. The theme switch in the masthead inverts the ladder; it does not introduce a colour.
Access
Skip link, one h1, landmarks and labelled controls; the ladder is a roving-focus toolbar with arrow, Home and End keys; the plates carry textual alternatives; text sits at 4.5:1 contrast or better in both themes; and every animation stops under prefers-reduced-motion.
