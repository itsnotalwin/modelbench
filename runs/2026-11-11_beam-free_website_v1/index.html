01 · Manifesto

Attention is not free. It is dragged.

We speak of attention as if it were a lamp — something we point, something we choose. It is not. Attention is a mass. It has inertia. It resists being moved, and once moved, it keeps moving until something heavier arrests it.

Weight

Every surface carries a centre of gravity. A headline pulls harder than a footnote because it is heavier, not because it is louder. Design is the art of placing weight where it earns its keep.

Drift

Left unattended, attention drifts along the path of least resistance — toward motion, toward novelty, toward the bright edge of the screen. Drift is not failure. It is physics.

Arrest

The designer's job is not to shout louder. It is to build a mass large enough, and placed well enough, to arrest the drift and hold it there — gently, honestly, for as long as it deserves.

“The eye does not wander. It falls — toward whatever is heaviest in the field.”

— From the field notes

02 · Principles

Six rules for building things that hold.

  • 01

    One centre of gravity

    Every composition gets one. If two elements compete for it, neither wins and the viewer leaves.

  • 02

    Contrast is mass

    Size, weight, spacing and silence are how you add mass. Colour is a crutch; we removed it.

  • 03

    Motion must earn its mass

    Animation is weight in time. If it does not carry meaning, it is friction dressed as delight.

  • 04

    Respect the inertia

    Once a user is moving, do not jerk them. Ease in, ease out, and never surprise without cause.

  • 05

    Silence is a surface

    Empty space is not absence. It is the heaviest element on the page, used deliberately.

  • 06

    Build for the long pull

    Performance, accessibility and clarity compound. Cleverness decays. Choose the long pull.
