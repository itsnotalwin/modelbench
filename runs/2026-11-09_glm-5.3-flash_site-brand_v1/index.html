Why the seed decides

A vegetable is a seed’s opinion of the future. Ours is this:

  1. 01

    Flavour was bred out on purpose.

    For seventy years, commercial breeding has selected for what a supermarket needs: uniform ripeness, thick skins, a shelf life measured in lorries. Taste was the cost, and it was paid quietly. Most of the varieties we grow were dropped from catalogues not because they failed, but because they bruised.

  2. 02

    Open-pollinated seed comes true — so it can be kept.

    F1 hybrids don’t breed straight: save their seed and you get a surprise. Ours come true, which is why gardeners have carried them forward for generations, selecting a little each year. Champion of England has been handed along since 1843 without a company ever owning it.

  3. 03

    Germination is a number, not a hope.

    Every batch is tested on damp kitchen roll in our shed-lab before it’s packed — 400 seeds, ten days, counted by hand. The result is on a certificate inside the packet. If a batch falls below what we declare, we replace it or refund it, your choice.

        How a packet is made

        Five steps, no shortcuts. The slow ones are the ones that matter.

        1. 01

          Grow

          Three smallholdings, four-year rotations, no synthetic inputs. We grow everything ourselves; we don’t repack anyone else’s seed.

        2. 02

          Rogue

          Through the season we pull anything off-type — a tall pea in a short row, a pale chard. What’s left is what comes true.

        3. 03

          Dry

          Harvested seed dries on racks in the barn until it’s below 15% moisture. Rushed seed dies quietly in the packet.

        4. 04

          Test

          400 seeds per batch on damp kitchen roll, ten days, counted by Sam by hand. The certificate in your packet is the same sheet.

        5. 05

          Pack

          Hand-packed in monthly batches and posted on Tuesdays. Packets are dated, not “best before” — seed is alive until it isn’t.

        Asked honestly, answered plainly

        Seven questions we get most. If yours isn’t here, it goes in the enquiry form below — and maybe in next year’s list.

        Why not just buy seed at the garden centre?

        You can, and for radishes you probably should. But most shelves carry F1 hybrids bred for uniformity, and a packet can’t tell you where its seed was grown or how it germinated. Ours can: batch number, testing date, and the certificate to match.

        Are your seeds organic?

        Grown to organic practice — rotations, no synthetic inputs — but not certified, because certification costs more than our turnover. We’d rather tell you plainly than wear a badge we haven’t earned.

        What if they don’t germinate?

        Every batch is tested before packing, and the certificate is in the packet. If your sowing fails well below that figure, write with the batch number and we’ll replace the packet or refund it — your choice, no photographs required. Bad weather isn’t covered; bad seed is.

        How many seeds are in a packet?

        It varies and we say so on every one: 24 for a beefsteak tomato, about 350 for carrots. We pack by count, not weight, so you’re never short-changed by light seed.

        Do you post outside the UK?

        No — UK addresses only. Legitimate seed movement needs paperwork we can’t do honestly at our size, and we won’t post in a jumper drawer.

        Can I save seed from your varieties?

        Please do. That’s the entire point of open-pollinated seed. Our saving guide is free with any order — a short one, because saving seed is simpler than the internet makes it look.

        Why is a packet £3.40 when the shed sells them for £2.99?

        Because ours were grown by three people here, tested here, and packed here — not traded. Roughly 40p of each packet is the germination test alone. We’re not the cheap option; we’re the accountable one.

            Germination guarantee

            Every batch we sell carries a certificate stating how it performed in our own test: 400 seeds on damp kitchen roll, counted by hand after ten days. The figure on the packet is the figure from that sheet.

            If your sowing fails well below the declared figure — say, under half of it, in reasonable conditions — write to us with the batch number and roughly what happened. We replace the packet or refund it, your choice, and we don’t ask for photographs of your soil.

            Bad weather isn’t covered; bad seed is. Storage matters too: seed kept dry and cool stays alive for years, and most of ours remain good well past the sow-by date on the certificate.

            Postage & returns

            Packets post on Tuesdays, first class, in paper-only packaging that goes straight on the compost. Postage is £3.20 flat for the UK, free over £25 — and the Sowing Order ships free, four times a year.

            Changed your mind? Unopened packets come back within 30 days for a full refund. Opened packets can’t be resold, so we don’t resell them — but if something is genuinely wrong with what arrived, tell us and we’ll make it right.

            We post to UK addresses only. See the questions above for why, and for what we do about seed that doesn’t come up.
