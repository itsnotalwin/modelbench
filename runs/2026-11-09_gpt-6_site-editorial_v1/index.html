01 / ORIGIN THE CITY ON THE RIDGE

A city that had to import its river.

Johannesburg's geography is the first clue to its water story. The city grew on the Witwatersrand, far from a major river. Springs, streams and wells could serve a mining camp; they could not comfortably serve a metropolis. Water infrastructure became part of the city's founding architecture, not an afterthought. [1][2]

“The city did not grow beside its water. It grew into the work of bringing water to it.”

The 1886 gold discovery brought a fast-growing settlement to a high ridge without a major river at its doorstep. Early supply depended on local streams, springs, wells and private waterworks. The water question arrived almost as quickly as the gold rush. [1]

02 / TRANSFER FOLLOW THE WATER

A drop crosses a border, then joins a system.

The Lesotho Highlands Water Project does not run a private pipe straight to a Johannesburg tap. It transfers water into the Vaal River System, where it joins other sources, is stored, abstracted, treated and sent onward. Select a numbered stop to follow the hand-offs. [3][5]

Field stop / Source01 / 07

The highlands hold the first reserve

In Lesotho, the Senqu/Orange catchment is captured and stored in a system of highland dams. Phase I centres on Katse and Mohale, linked by tunnels and transfer works.

The water is not yet Johannesburg's drinking water. It is raw water entering a larger river system.

The important distinction: the Lesotho Highlands Water Project augments the Vaal River System. It does not replace that system, and it is not a dedicated line carrying a separately identifiable “Lesotho drop” to every home. [3]

Two countries, shared infrastructure: the 1986 treaty established a multi-phase project that supplies water to Gauteng and generates hydropower for Lesotho. The benefits and the obligations cross the border together. [4]

03 / TRANSFORMATION INSIDE THE WORKS

Clear water is made, not found.

Raw water carries suspended particles and microorganisms. A treatment works turns it into water that can safely travel through a long distribution network. Choose a stage to see what changes, and why the order matters. [6][14]

04 / LAST MILE THE CITY'S BUFFER

A reservoir is a pause with a clock inside.

Treated water still has to reach a neighbourhood. Municipal reservoirs and towers hold a buffer between bulk supply and the uneven rhythm of household demand. Their published storage hours offer a useful way to understand how different parts of a city have different amounts of breathing room. [8]

Johannesburg Water's published overview lists 128 reservoirs and water towers and 12,364 kilometres of water pipes across the municipal network. Those are not just statistics about scale. They describe the final, complicated leg between a bulk supplier's reservoir and the tap in a specific street. [7]

A municipal reservoir is a buffer, not a source. It can smooth the mismatch between steady inflow and lumpy demand: morning showers, a hot afternoon, a school opening, a pump that needs maintenance. When inflow falters or draw-off rises, the buffer begins to shrink.

Pressure is shared

In a joint statement in November 2024, the national department, Gauteng, the City and Rand Water described the chain clearly: Rand Water buys raw water from the Department of Water and Sanitation, treats it and supplies bulk water; Johannesburg Water buys that treated water and distributes it through municipal reservoirs and pipes. The same statement cited the 2023 No Drop report's estimate of 35% water losses in Johannesburg. In October 2026, Johannesburg Water again described high demand and excessive draw-off as constraints on reservoir recovery. Different points in the chain can become the bottleneck; the tap only tells you that pressure has arrived, or has not. [9][10]

05 / CONCLUSION WHAT THE TAP CANNOT TELL YOU

The last metre is never just a metre.

At the tap, the whole system becomes invisible. The mountain catchment is gone. So are the treaty, the tunnel, the river releases, the treatment tanks, the pumps and the municipal storage calculations. What remains is a handle, a pipe and a glass.

But a reliable tap is not a single machine working perfectly. It is a sequence of institutions and physical systems that have to keep agreeing with one another: a source with enough water, an allocation that respects the resource, treatment capacity, electricity, functioning pumps, storage, sound pipes and demand that the network can carry.

Johannesburg's water story is often told as a story of distance. Distance matters. Yet the more revealing story is one of connection: a city far from a river made itself possible by building relationships across watersheds, borders and neighbourhoods. Its future depends on maintaining those relationships, not merely extending the next pipe.

“A tap is the final punctuation mark in a sentence written across a landscape.”

FIELD LEXICON WORDS THAT MOVE WATER

A small language for a large system.

The infrastructure has its own vocabulary. Search a term to find the idea hiding inside it.

10 TERMS

Catchment

An area of land where rainfall and runoff drain towards a common river, dam or outlet. The boundary is made by topography, not a municipal map.

Transfer

The engineered movement of water from one catchment or storage system to another. In this story, the highlands transfer augments the Vaal system.

Abstraction

Taking raw water from a river or dam for use. It is a regulated act, not an unlimited right to draw whatever the pumps can lift.

Bulk water

Treated water supplied in large volumes to municipalities or other customers, before the local network delivers it to individual properties.

Coagulation

A treatment step that destabilises very fine suspended particles so they can begin to gather into larger clumps.

Floc

The visible, soft clusters formed as particles gather during treatment. Gentle mixing helps them grow large enough to settle out.

Sedimentation

The settling of heavier floc under gravity in a tank, leaving clearer water above for the next treatment stage.

Storage hours

A planning measure of how long a reservoir's stated storage volume can meet a reference demand. It is not a live reading of how full the tank is.

Pressure zone

A part of a distribution network managed around elevation and pressure limits. Pumps, valves and reservoirs help move water through it.

Non-revenue water

Water that enters a system but is not billed as authorised consumption. It can include physical losses, meter inaccuracies and unbilled authorised use.

No term found. Try a shorter word or a different part of the system.
