S.01 — Why long

A loop is a lie
told smoothly.

Your ear clocks a looped ambience in about ninety seconds. After that it’s wallpaper — and wallpaper is the enemy of whatever you’re making: a harbour montage, a five-minute game vista, a Tuesday that needs to feel like it is actually raining somewhere.

So Longshore records long. The minimum take is four hours; the longest so far is eleven. Nothing is layered, nothing is looped, nothing is stitched. When the tide comes in on track seven, that is because the tide came in.

The only processing is gain. What reached the microphones is what reaches your timeline — at 24-bit / 96 kHz, logged with grid reference, tide state and weather, so you always know exactly what you are hearing, and what heard it first.

  • No loops

    The tide comes in because the tide came in.

  • No layers

    One place, one hour, one file. That’s the whole recipe.

  • No rescue

    If a take needed saving, it isn’t for sale.

S.04 — Method

How a place
becomes a file.

Four steps, none of them optional. This is the whole method — there is no secret part.

  1. 01

    The rig

    A pair of matched omnidirectional capsules in serious wind protection, spaced thirty centimetres apart, into a recorder that writes 24-bit / 96 kHz to two cards at once. If gusts at the capsule exceed six metres per second, we pack up: the take is already worthless, and no windsock on earth will save it. The sea doesn’t need a filter. Neither do you.

    LOG 0141 — GUSTS 7.2 M/S.
    PACKED UP AT 06:05.
  2. 02

    The wait

    Everything is scheduled off the tide table and the synoptic chart. Marshes on the flooding tide; shingle in the falling swell after a gale; the harbour when the fleet is in and the winches are warm. The minimum take is four hours, because the first hour is spent learning to listen, and the second is where the good surprises live.

    LOG 0148 — ON STATION 04:51.
    FLOOD DUE 06:32.
  3. 03

    The log

    Every take is logged the same evening: grid reference, tide state, weather, gain, and anything that happened — a walker, an outboard, the recorder’s own noise. Flaws are not hidden. They are printed on the product page, priced into the number, and left in the file, because a cut at a zero-crossing is where honesty goes to die.

    LOG 0152 — 41 ENTRIES,
    3 FLAWS KEPT, 0 HIDDEN.
  4. 04

    The licence

    One licence, unlimited projects, forever. Broadcast needs a cue sheet and nothing else. Studios of five or fewer are covered by the same single licence; above that, tell us your shape and we’ll quote inside a working day. There is no per-project fee, there will never be a subscription you didn’t choose, and your licence PDF has your name on it.

S.06 — Who is behind it

The person holding
the microphones.

Mirren Calder was a bioacoustics researcher — nine years counting seabirds for a living, first in Shetland, then on the Bass Rock — before she admitted that what she wanted to keep was the sound the counting happened inside of. Longshore is what that admission became: one person, one coast, a serious recorder, and a firm opinion about loops.

She records, edits, logs, invoices, and answers this website’s email herself. Support is the founder — which is either a red flag or the whole point, depending on how many vendors you have emailed this month.

S.07 — Objections

Doubts, answered properly.

Can I use these in something that makes money?

Yes — that is the default. One licence covers commercial work: film, television, games, installations, advertising, apps, whatever the future invents. You don’t tell us the project, you don’t clear it twice, and you don’t pay again for the next one.

What about broadcast?

A cue sheet, that’s it. List “Longshore (M. Calder)” as source and publisher on the programme’s cue sheet, exactly as you would for library music. No PRO, no per-second rate, no additional fee — the licence you already have is the broadcast licence.

Why not just record it myself?

Genuinely — you should, some of it. But budget a location day, permits for the reserves, wind insurance, fourteen hours of tide patience and a second pair of hands, then ask what your day is worth. Or spend £34 and get the afternoon back.

What exactly arrives when I pay?

A download link within minutes: 24-bit / 96 kHz WAVs, take by take, plus MP3s for auditioning and a CSV of the log. No account, no portal, no dongle. Updates to a library you own are free, forever, and arrive by email with a changelog written like a log, because it is one.

What if a file is flawed?

Then it said so before you paid. Every known flaw is printed on the product row — outboard engines, car doors, one self-noise spike — and the price already carries it. If you find something the log missed, you get the library fixed or your money back, and the log gets a correction in the next release.

Refunds?

Thirty days, full stop. Reply to the receipt with “no, thanks” and the money returns the way it came. No form, no interrogation, no “please tell us what would make this right for you.”

Do you take commissions?

Yes. Bespoke location recording from £600 per day plus expenses — tide and weather permitting, anywhere the road goes. Tell us the place and the feeling; we’ll tell you honestly whether it’s recordable, and how many attempts the weather usually takes.

Privacy, briefly

This site runs entirely in your browser. It loads no analytics, sets no cookies, fetches no fonts, and reports to nobody.

The enquiry form keeps a working draft on this device so a reload can’t eat your sentences — nothing is transmitted anywhere. Press send, and the enquiry is processed locally; your copy downloads from the same page.

If you email us the old-fashioned way, your address is used to reply, then kept until the thread is done. That is the entire policy. The founder reads it, so it is short enough to read.

Licence terms, plainly

One licence covers one purchaser — a person, or a team of five or fewer — on unlimited projects, worldwide, in perpetuity. Film, television, games, installations, advertising, apps: all included, no per-project fees, no re-clearing.

Broadcast: file a cue sheet naming “Longshore (M. Calder)” as source and publisher. That is the whole obligation.
