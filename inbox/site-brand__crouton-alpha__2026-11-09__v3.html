Why chefs switch — and why they stay

You buy produce with your prep time in mind. Our box is built that way. The quantity is set by covers, not by a “medium” that means something different every week. The herbs are bunched by portion, the leaves are washed once, and the beet is sized for the mandoline you actually own. If a line is wrong, you swap it before four and it does not cost you a phone call.

We are not a wholesaler and we are not a farm shop. We are a kitchen supply partner who happens to grow most of what we send. That means we can tell you why the celeriac is small this week — a dry fortnight in June — and what we are doing about it, because we are the people who saw it happen. A wholesaler will tell you the celeriac is “in”. We will tell you it is a 400-gram celeriac and you should plan soup.

Our customers are not buying virtue. They are buying reliability on a Thursday. The sustainability is real, audited and printed on the box, but nobody in a kitchen has time for a lecture at pass. The box works because the food is good and the timing is exact, and the ethics ride along.

Who it is for

  • High-street restaurants doing 40 to 80 covers who want seasonality without chaos.
  • Hotels and pubs with a set menu and a variable week.
  • Staff canteens and care homes that need the same reliability at a different price.

We do not supply single households, and we do not do one-off “special occasion” boxes. If you need that, a good farm shop is a better answer than us.

How it is grown — on three farms you can name

We farm 47 hectares across three holdings in East Anglia, no-till, with livestock rotated through the vegetable ground in winter. That sentence could be a slogan, so here is what it costs us: we yield about 15% less than a conventional grower in a wet year, and we take that loss because the soil is the asset. The box price reflects it. If you want the cheapest carrot in the county, we are not your carrot.

Field 1 — Norwich Road

Chard, kale, squash. The ground leans sandy, so we keep the cover crop on until the last frost. The chard is cut, not pulled, and it comes back twice.

Field 2 — Bungay

Beet, celeriac, leeks. Heavier soil, slower drainage. The beet is direct-sown in April and thinned by hand, which is why the sizes are even enough for a mandoline.

Field 3 — Beccles

Herbs, salad and flowers. The herbs are cut at dawn and are in the box by nine. In high summer the whole box smells like a greenhouse that has been opened.

Proof from kitchens, not from us

At The Anchor, Southwold, a 72-cover restaurant, the switch was prompted by a missed delivery from their wholesaler on a Friday. The first Plough & Pulse box arrived the next Thursday, with the chard sized for the de long menu. Within a month, the head chef had rewritten the autumn menu around the squash we said would be abundant, because we had told them in July that it would be. That forecast, and being right, is the whole business.

At Greenhouse Hotel, near Diss, the gain was in waste. The hotel kitchen was throwing away a quarter of a wholesale delivery because it was packed for a larger kitchen. With covers-based quantities, waste fell to under a tenth in eight weeks, according to their own weigh-in. They did not buy our box to be sustainable. They bought it to stop paying for food they binned.

We publish our miss rate. It is 0.4% of lines, and when we miss, the swap is free and the credit is automatic. If we cannot grow it, we say so at the menu note stage, not at the door. A chef who is told early will forgive a short box. A chef who is surprised will not.

Build your box — see the week

Set covers, pick a style, and the box size, price and menu ideas update together. The price is per delivery, twice a week, and includes the menu note and the return of the box. If you add a third delivery, the box gets smaller and fresher, which is the entire point.

What is in every box

Three leaves, two roots, two herbs, one surprise that is never a surprise because it is listed on the menu note. The surprise is edible, seasonal and always smaller than the chard. Allergies and equipment are on the customer profile, so we do not send the mandoline beet to the kitchen without the mandoline.

Questions from the pass

What if we do not use a line? Swap it before 16:00 the day before, by replying to the menu note. It costs nothing and the box is re-packed. If you forget, the line rolls forward and you are not charged for it twice.

Is the price fixed? The box price is fixed per season, and the season is set by the cost of growing, not by the wholesale market. If a dry summer shortens a line, the price does not rise. The quantity adjusts and we tell you.

Can we visit? Yes. The first Saturday of each month is a farm walk for chefs and buyers. You will see the rotation, the wash line and the cold store, and you will leave with more questions than you arrived with, which is the point.

Delivery and returns

Boxes arrive in food-grade crates that we collect and wash. The deposit is included in the first invoice and returned when the last crate comes back. We do not use single-use plastic. If a crate is lost, it is £8, which is the cost of the crate and not a penalty for being busy.
