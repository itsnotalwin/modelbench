What we sell — and who buys it

We sell time. A permanent sea wall takes planning consent, a design team, and a winter you do not have. TideLock buys you the season. Our customers are harbour authorities with a town hall they cannot flood, councils with a pumping station that must stay dry, and developers who need a site protected while the permanent work is consented.

We are not a civil engineering contractor. We do not pour concrete, and we do not pretend our barrier is a permanent solution. If you need a hundred-year asset, hire an engineer. If you need a wall up before Thursday’s spring tide, call us. That distinction is honest, and it is why our barriers are designed to be inspected, reused and moved — not built and forgotten.

Every system is three parts: posts fixed to a reusable foundation, aluminium panels that slot between them, and a gasket that seals to the ground. Nothing is welded in place. A forklift can undo in an afternoon what it took a crane to put up in a morning. The panels stack on a lorry, the posts travel on a trailer, and the engineering file travels with every hire.

How deployment actually works

We run deployment like a fire drill, because in a surge it will be one. The timeline below is real, measured on our last three deployments, not a brochure promise.

Hour 0

Survey and set-out

Two surveyors, one level, one toughbook. We mark post positions and check ground levels. On grass or made ground we use ground screws. On quay concrete we use resin anchors. No drilling into live services — the desk study and the CAT scanner do that work first.

Hour 2

Posts and foundations

Posts go up at two-metre spacing. A three-person crew sets about eight posts an hour on prepared ground. Ballast blocks, where needed, are placed by forklift. The crew leader signs each post against the drawing before panels are released from the lorry.

Hour 6

Panels, seals and test

Panels slot and lock, gaskets compress, and we run a hose test along the toe. The sign-off sheet is photographed and sent to the duty officer the same hour. If the forecast improves, we can strike the wall in half the time it took to build it.

Proof, not promises

We publish what happened at every deployment, including the ones that nearly went wrong. In Whitstable, 2023, a surge arrived four hours early while a panel was mid-lift. The crew paused, bolted the temporary strand, and the town stayed dry. The incident report is seven pages and we will send it to you before you sign anything.

In Morecambe, 2024, we deployed 140 metres in nine hours on a rising tide, on grass, with a crew of nine. The wall held for three tides and was struck in four hours the following week. The harbour authority commissioned the same system again in 2025, this time as a seasonal lease, because their risk committee preferred the option value of not owning a wall that only matters three days a year.

Our insurance is not a slogan. Every panel has a serial number. Every deployment has a torque log. If a gasket fails, we know which batch it was and we can tell you whether any other wall in the country has the same batch. That level of traceability is expensive, and it is also why customers can defend the decision to buy from us in a committee meeting.

Questions we answer every week

Is it a flood defence or a flood barrier? A barrier. It is temporary, removable and engineered to a stated surge. If you need a permanent defence, we will tell you to get a chartered engineer and we will happily supply the temporary cover while that happens.

What if the forecast is wrong? Then you have a wall you did not need, which is the best kind of wrong. The lease model exists because the cost of being wrong about not building is worse than the cost of not needing it.

Can it stay up all winter? Yes, with a monthly inspection. Panels and gaskets are rated for a full season, and the inspection log is part of the lease.

More answers

Will it damage the quay? Anchors are resin-set and removable. We restore holes and provide a method statement for the asset owner.

Who operates it? We deploy and strike, or we train your crew and supervise the first deployment. Either way, the torque log is yours.

What about planning? Temporary structures for flood response are generally permitted development for the duration of the risk, but check with your authority. We will provide drawings and a structural report.

Send us the length, the ground and the surge level you are worried about. A named engineer replies within one working day, with a drawing and a price that matches the configurator. If we are not the right answer, we will say so and tell you what is.
